CONCEPT

What is a Man-in-the-Middle Attack?

A MITM attack occurs when an attacker positions themselves between two communicating parties and can observe, relay, or modify traffic. The key security weakness demonstrated here is lack of authentication.

📡

Interception

The attacker gets between two parties and relays their communication.

🔑

Key Exchange Risk

Unauthenticated key exchange can allow an attacker to establish separate keys with both parties.

🔒

Authentication

Certificates, digital signatures and authenticated key exchange help prevent impersonation.

DEFENSE

How to Prevent MITM Attacks

Security controls should provide both confidentiality and authentication.

🔐 TLS/HTTPS

Use authenticated TLS connections and validate certificates.

📜 Digital Certificates

Certificates bind identities to public keys through trusted authorities.

✍️ Digital Signatures

Signatures can authenticate messages and detect unauthorized modification.

🔑 Authenticated Key Exchange

Use protocols that authenticate the parties during key establishment.

🛜 Secure Networks

Avoid untrusted networks and use secure Wi-Fi configurations.

⚠️ Certificate Warnings

Do not ignore browser or application warnings about invalid certificates.

ATTACK FLOW

MITM Attack Lifecycle

01

Position

Attacker gets between two communicating parties.

02

Intercept

Traffic is captured and relayed through the attacker.

03

Observe or Modify

The attacker may read or alter traffic depending on the protocol and protections.

04

Detect / Prevent

Authentication, integrity checks and secure protocols stop or expose the attack.

ACADEMIC NOTE

Confidentiality Is Not Enough

Encryption can protect the contents of a message from passive observers, but an endpoint still needs assurance about who it is communicating with. MITM attacks demonstrate why confidentiality, integrity and authentication must be considered together.
